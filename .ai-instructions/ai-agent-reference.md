## Research Protocols by Topic

### Protocol 1: Megalithic Engineering Research

**Scope**: Sites with massive stone blocks (>20 tons), long-distance transport, or extreme weight anomalies.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Identify Specifications**
   - Stone weight (tons)
   - Dimensions (length × width × height)
   - Material type and Mohs hardness
   - Quarry location and transport distance
   - Elevation changes in transport

2. **Analyze Engineering Challenges**
   - Calculate volume and weight
   - Assess transport difficulty (terrain, distance, elevation)
   - Evaluate lifting and placement requirements
   - Consider tool technology available at time period

3. **Document Evidence**
   - Archaeological dating methods
   - Construction technique evidence
   - Tool marks and working methods
   - Modern replication attempts
   - Transportation theories

4. **Compare Across Sites**
   - Cross-cultural similarities
   - Timeline considerations
   - Technique patterns
   - Scale comparisons

**Key Questions to Address**:
- What is the largest stone and its specifications?
- How far was it transported and what terrain?
- What elevation changes were involved?
- What tools/techniques are evidenced?
- What do modern experts say about feasibility?
- Have replication attempts been successful?

**Documentation Files**: megalithic-engineering.md, construction-phases.md

### Protocol 2: Precision Stonework Research

**Scope**: Sites with micron-level accuracy, mirror finishes, or tolerances exceeding hand-tool capability.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Measure Precision**
   - Surface flatness (microns)
   - Angle accuracy (degrees/arcminutes)
   - Circularity/symmetry errors
   - Interior/exterior tolerances
   - Finish quality (surface roughness Ra value)

2. **Material Analysis**
   - Stone type and Mohs hardness
   - Working difficulty
   - Tool requirements for precision achieved
   - Comparison to modern capabilities

3. **Technology Assessment**
   - What technology would be required today?
   - What tools are evidenced in archaeological record?
   - Technology gap analysis
   - CNC/machining equivalents

4. **Dating Context**
   - When was it made?
   - What was technology level at that time?
   - Inscription vs. precision work relationship
   - Multi-phase construction evidence

**Key Questions to Address**:
- What is the measured precision (in microns/millimeters)?
- What stone type and hardness?
- What modern technology equivalent?
- What tools are evidenced archaeologically?
- Are inscriptions present? What quality compared to precision work?
- Is there weathering differential or multi-phase evidence?

**Documentation Files**: precision-stonework.md, predynastic-vases.md, inscription-dating-problem.md

### Protocol 3: OOPArts (Out-of-Place Artifacts) Research

**Scope**: Artifacts that appear anachronistic for their time period or context.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Verify Authenticity**
   - Provenance documentation
   - Chain of custody
   - Professional archaeological context
   - Museum/academic validation
   - Debunking check

2. **Analyze Anomaly**
   - What makes it "out of place"?
   - Technology level vs. time period
   - Material vs. expected capability
   - Function vs. known uses

3. **Evaluate Explanations**
   - Conventional archaeological explanation
   - Alternative theories
   - Modern replication feasibility
   - Technology gap assessment

4. **Source Quality**
   - Peer-reviewed publications
   - Museum documentation
   - Academic consensus
   - Fringe vs. mainstream sources

**Key Questions to Address**:
- Is provenance verified?
- What makes it anomalous?
- Has it been debunked?
- What do mainstream archaeologists say?
- Are there modern equivalents?
- What is the technology gap?

**Common Debunked OOPArts to Avoid**:
- Crystal skulls (19th century fabrications)
- Coso Artifact (1920s spark plug in concretion)
- Dropa Stones (hoax)
- Ancient astronaut claims without evidence

**Documentation Files**: ooparts.md, ancient-knowledge.md

### Protocol 4: Ancient Knowledge Research

**Scope**: Advanced knowledge of astronomy, mathematics, cartography, or science.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Document Knowledge**
   - Specific achievement (e.g., eclipse prediction)
   - Accuracy measurement
   - Time period and culture
   - How knowledge was recorded/transmitted

2. **Analyze Sophistication**
   - Compare to modern calculations
   - Required observations and calculations
   - Technology requirements
   - Knowledge prerequisites

3. **Evaluate Context**
   - How did they acquire this knowledge?
   - What tools/methods are evidenced?
   - Transmission mechanisms
   - Knowledge loss circumstances

4. **Verify Claims**
   - Separate fact from speculation
   - Check mathematical accuracy
   - Validate historical sources
   - Distinguish interpretation from evidence

**Key Questions to Address**:
- What specific knowledge is demonstrated?
- How accurate is it compared to modern?
- What evidence exists for the knowledge?
- How could they have acquired it?
- Is there evidence of the methods used?
- How was it transmitted/preserved?

**Documentation Files**: ancient-knowledge.md

### Protocol 5: Geoglyphs and Earthworks Research

**Scope**: Large-scale ground designs, mounds, and aerial-perspective features.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Document Scale and Design**
   - Dimensions (length, width, area)
   - Design complexity
   - Visibility conditions (ground vs. aerial)
   - Alignment features

2. **Analyze Construction**
   - Material volume
   - Construction time estimates
   - Labor requirements
   - Maintenance evidence

3. **Evaluate Purpose**
   - Astronomical alignments
   - Ritual/religious function
   - Territorial markers
   - Agricultural connections

4. **Geographic Context**
   - Regional patterns
   - Cultural connections
   - Time period and culture
   - Climate and preservation

**Key Questions to Address**:
- What are dimensions and scale?
- How was it constructed?
- What is its purpose/function?
- Are there astronomical alignments?
- Why visible primarily from air?
- What culture and time period?

**Documentation Files**: geoglyphs.md

### Protocol 6: Construction Phases Research

**Scope**: Sites showing degradation pattern where oldest = most sophisticated.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Identify Phases**
   - Layer/phase descriptions
   - Dating for each phase
   - Construction quality by phase
   - Weathering by phase

2. **Quantify Degradation**
   - Measure block sizes by phase
   - Precision measurements by phase
   - Weathering measurements
   - Technique changes

3. **Analyze Pattern**
   - Does oldest = most sophisticated?
   - Quantitative degradation metrics
   - Cross-site comparison
   - Timeline analysis

4. **Evaluate Explanations**
   - Conventional (resource depletion, motivation)
   - Alternative (lost techniques, catastrophic loss)
   - Evidence for each
   - Testable predictions

**Key Questions to Address**:
- Are multiple construction phases evident?
- How are phases dated?
- Which phase has largest/most precise work?
- What weathering pattern is observed?
- Does pattern match expected technological development?
- What explains the degradation?

**Documentation Files**: construction-phases.md, inscription-dating-problem.md

### Protocol 7: Inscription Dating Problem Research

**Scope**: Sites where crude inscriptions are used to date high-precision work.

**Standard Research Steps**:
1. **Separate Technologies**
   - Identify precision/megalithic work
   - Identify inscription work
   - Measure quality of each
   - Tool evidence for each

2. **Analyze Relationship**
   - Are inscriptions integral or added?
   - Quality comparison (precision vs. crude)
   - Weathering differential
   - Tool mark differences

3. **Evaluate Dating**
   - How is site dated?
   - Is dating based on inscriptions?
   - Is precision work dated independently?
   - Circular reasoning check

4. **Document Evidence**
   - Uninscribed precision artifacts
   - Weathering paradoxes
   - Multi-phase evidence
   - Provenance of dating

**Key Questions to Address**:
- Is precision work inscribed?
- If inscribed, what is inscription quality vs. precision quality?
- Is there weathering differential?
- How is the site dated?
- Is dating circular (proximity = attribution)?
- What would resolve the dating?

**Documentation Files**: inscription-dating-problem.md, construction-phases.md, precision-stonework.md

## Tag Taxonomy and Meanings

### Construction Techniques
| Tag | Meaning | Examples |
|-----|---------|----------|
| `polygonal_masonry` | Irregularly-shaped interlocking stones fitted without mortar | Sacsayhuamán, Ollantaytambo, Wall of Arwad |
| `cyclopean_masonry` | Massive stone blocks without mortar (Cyclopean = myth-level scale) | Baalbek, Sacsayhuamán |
| `precision_fitting` | Joints so tight paper cannot fit between | Sacsayhuamán, Puma Punku, Valley Temple |
| `mortise_tenon_joinery` | Stones joined using projecting tenons fitting into mortise holes | Stonehenge, Puma Punku |
| `metal_clamps` | Metal connectors joining stones | Ollantaytambo, Tiwanaku, Greek temples |
| `modular_construction` | Standardized, interchangeable components | Puma Punku H-blocks, predynastic vases |
| `corner_first_construction` | Corners built first, then fill | Great Pyramid casing |

### Precision Categories
| Tag | Meaning | Precision Level | Examples |
|-----|---------|----------------|----------|
| `micron_level_precision` | Tolerances <100 microns (0.1mm) | 0.4-50µm | Predynastic vases (15-50µm), Serapeum boxes (5µm) |
| `submillimeter_precision` | Tolerances <1mm | 0.1-1mm | Puma Punku interior channels (1mm) |
| `millimeter_precision` | Tolerances 1-10mm | 1-10mm | Puma Punku flat surfaces (1-2mm) |
| `mirror_polish` | Surface finish approaching optical quality | Ra <1µm | Barabar Caves (Ra <0.1µm), Serapeum boxes |
| `cnc_equivalent` | Precision requiring modern CNC to replicate | Variable | Predynastic vases, Serapeum boxes, Puma Punku H-blocks |

## Common Research Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Source Quality Errors
**Problem**: Using low-quality sources as if they're authoritative.

**Avoid**:
- Citing YouTube videos as primary sources
- Treating fringe books as peer-reviewed
- Using tabloid articles for technical claims
- Accepting unverified provenance

**Solution**: Always classify sources by tier (see ai-research-methodology.md) and weight accordingly.

### Pitfall 2: Measurement Confusion
**Problem**: Confusing precision types or units.

**Avoid**:
- Mixing flatness with parallelism
- Confusing microns (µm) with millimeters (mm)
- Stating "perfect" without quantification
- Omitting measurement method

**Solution**: Always include: measurement type, value with units, method, and who measured.

### Pitfall 3: Circular Dating
**Problem**: Using inscriptions to date precision work that may predate inscriptions.

**Avoid**:
- Assuming inscriptions are contemporaneous with structure
- Ignoring weathering differentials
- Dismissing multi-phase evidence
- Accepting proximity = attribution

**Solution**: Separate precision work from inscription work. Note dating methods and potential circularity.

### Pitfall 4: Technology Anachronism
**Problem**: Claiming "impossible" without proper analysis.

**Avoid**:
- "Impossible with copper tools" (needs experimental verification)
- "Required aliens/Atlanteans" (leap without evidence)
- "No way ancient people could..." (underestimates human ingenuity)
- Ignoring modern replication attempts

**Solution**: State what technology would be required today, what's evidenced archaeologically, and the gap between them. Avoid absolute claims.

### Pitfall 5: Cherry-Picking Evidence
**Problem**: Presenting only evidence supporting one view.

**Avoid**:
- Citing only alternative researchers
- Ignoring mainstream explanations
- Omitting failed replication attempts
- Hiding contradictory evidence

**Solution**: Present both conventional and alternative views. Acknowledge strengths and weaknesses of each.

### Pitfall 6: Sensationalism
**Problem**: Using exaggerated language that undermines credibility.

**Avoid**:
- "Impossible", "aliens", "lost civilization" without evidence
- "Proves ancient advanced technology"
- "Archaeologists baffled" (rarely accurate)
- "Ancient astronauts" without extremely strong evidence

**Solution**: Use measured language. "Anomalous", "unexplained by conventional theories", "precision exceeding expected capabilities".

### Pitfall 7: Debunked Claims
**Problem**: Repeating claims that have been thoroughly debunked.

**Avoid**:
- Crystal skulls (19th century fabrications)
- Coso Artifact (1920s spark plug)
- Dropa Stones (hoax)
- Abydos Helicopter (pareidolia from palimpsest)
- Iron Pillar of Delhi as "rust-proof" (it does rust, just slowly)

**Solution**: Check mainstream archaeology sources before citing controversial claims.

### Pitfall 8: False Dichotomy
**Problem**: Presenting only two options when more exist.

**Avoid**:
- "Either copper tools or power tools" (ignores abrasives, time, techniques)
- "Either Egyptians or lost civilization" (ignores Egyptians with lost techniques)
- "Natural or artificial" (ignores natural features modified by humans)

**Solution**: Consider full range of possibilities. Acknowledge intermediate positions.

### Pitfall 9: Misunderstanding Archaeology
**Problem**: Assuming absence of evidence = evidence of absence.

**Avoid**:
- "No tools found = no tools existed" (organic materials decay)
- "No writing = no knowledge" (oral transmission)
- "No evidence = didn't happen" (preservation bias)

**Solution**: Acknowledge limitations of archaeological record. Note what could have been lost.

### Pitfall 10: Inappropriate Comparisons
**Problem**: Comparing unlike things as if equivalent.

**Avoid**:
- Comparing best ancient work to average modern work
- Modern hobby attempts vs. professional ancient specialists
- Short-term experiments vs. multi-generational projects
- Different materials without accounting for properties

**Solution**: Ensure comparisons are fair and appropriate. Compare best to best, account for time and specialization.
